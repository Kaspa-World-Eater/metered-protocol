1b. RATE AGAINST CHUNK SIZE, at EQUAL chunk counts
     The comparison only means something if every size gets the same number of chunk
     BOUNDARIES, because a boundary is where divergence can happen. An earlier version
     of this section generated equal numbers of TEXTS, which gave size 3 over a hundred
     times the sample of size 550 and made large chunks look clean when they were only
     under-observed. Where a count is zero, the 95% upper bound is the rule of three,
     3/n -- absence of evidence, priced.
     chunk   chunks   non-zero   rate       95% upper bound
         3     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%
         5     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%
        11     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%
        23     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%
        50     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%
       200     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%
       550     4000          0   0.0000%   0.0750%

     Divergence is RARE AT EVERY SIZE, of order 1e-4, and this sample cannot
     resolve a difference between sizes. The honest conclusion is a bound, not a
     floor: nothing here justifies a minimum babelUnits, and nothing here rules one
     out either. What it does establish is that the honest rate is small enough that
     the detector below is not fighting it.

2. WHAT A BUYER STEALS, per SPEC.md 5.1's threat: under-report by 1 token
     honest drift            : +0.000000 tokens/chunk
     a buyer cheating 100%   : +1.000000 tokens/chunk
     a buyer cheating  50%   : +0.500000 tokens/chunk
     a buyer cheating  25%   : +0.250000 tokens/chunk
     a buyer cheating  10%   : +0.100000 tokens/chunk
     The honest drift is zero to measurement, so ANY positive drift is signal.
     That is the good case for a detector -- and section 3 shows it still is not
     enough, because a rare cheat is small as well as one-sided.

3. DETECTOR: one-sided CUSUM, k=0.5, h=5  (k above honest drift, below any cheat)
     FALSE ALARMS on honest sessions : 0 / 400 sessions of 2000 chunks

     detection of a buyer under-reporting 1 token on a fraction of chunks:
     leak rate   detected   median chunks to alarm   cost to buyer
        100%     100.0%        9                  0.182% of a 550-token chunk
         50%     100.0%       80                  0.091% of a 550-token chunk
         25%       1.0%     1499                  0.045% of a 550-token chunk
         10%       0.0%        -                  0.018% of a 550-token chunk
          5%       0.0%        -                  0.009% of a 550-token chunk

4. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR 5.1
     The detector works, and against the exact threat 5.1 names. A buyer under-
     reporting one token on EVERY chunk -- the 0.18% leak 5.1 was written to stop --
     is caught in a median of 9 chunks, with zero false alarms in 800,000 honest ones.
     'Persistent one-sided residual' becomes CUSUM(k=0.5, h=5), which two implementers
     can read the same way.

     What it does NOT catch is a patient buyer. At 25% of chunks detection collapses
     to 1%, and at 10% it never fires. So 5.1's claim that it converts an unbounded
     leak into a bounded one is TRUE, and the bound is now a number rather than a
     hope: roughly 0.045% per chunk, an order of magnitude under the threat named,
     but NOT zero. 5.1 should say so, because a reader currently infers zero.

     Honest drift being zero is why any of this works, and it also corrects Finding D
     earlier: the fear that this detector would halt honest sessions is not
     supported at realistic chunk sizes. Study A's counter-examples were mostly at
     chunk=5. What survives of Finding D is that 'persistent' was undefined -- which
     these numbers now fix.
